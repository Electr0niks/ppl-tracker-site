How to choose a workout tracker app.

A good tracker should save you time on every set, not cost you time. Here are the criteria that actually matter, and the traps to avoid.

By Julien, developer of PPL Tracker · Updated November 19, 2026

The criteria that matter.

  • Fast logging: enter weight and reps in seconds between sets, without digging through menus.
  • Previous session visible: see what you did last time while you do your set. That's what drives progress.
  • A rest timer that starts on its own.
  • PRs and progress: know at a glance whether you're moving forward.
  • The exercises you actually do, with variations to choose from.

Traps to check before installing.

  • The hidden subscription: a "free" app that locks the essentials (timer, history, number of routines) behind a monthly plan. Check what the free version really does.
  • Mandatory accounts: not needed to log sets. If you want sync across devices, though, you'll need one.
  • Personal data: on Google Play, each listing's "Data safety" section shows what is collected and shared.
  • Ads between sets that break your rhythm.
  • Connectivity: an app that doesn't work offline is a pain in a basement gym.

FAQ.

Is a free app enough?

Yes, if the free version lets you log sessions, see the previous one and keep your full history. Paid features are mostly advanced stats.

Subscription or one-time purchase?

A one-time purchase costs less over time. A subscription can make sense for online services (sync, coaching).

Does a paper log work too?

Yes. An app saves time comparing to last session, tracking PRs and volume, but the key is to log.
