Starting PPL: your first week.

The hardest part is starting without getting hurt or discouraged. Here is how to approach your first week of PPL, session by session.

By Julien, developer of PPL Tracker · Updated November 19, 2026

Before your first session.

  • Pick your schedule: 3 sessions a week (Push Monday, Pull Wednesday, Legs Friday) is perfect to start.
  • Grab the program: the beginner PPL program lists the exercises, sets and rest.
  • Plan to log everything: weight and reps for every set, from day one. That's your baseline.

Finding your starting weights.

Week one is for calibrating, not setting records:

  1. Start very light and do 8 to 10 clean reps.
  2. Add a little weight each set as long as your form stays clean.
  3. Stop at the weight that leaves you 2 or 3 reps in reserve at the top of the range.
  4. Write that weight down: it's your starting point for next week.

Already have an idea of your strength? The 1RM calculator gives suggested weights per rep count.

The sample week.

DaySessionGoal
MondayPushCalibrate bench and overhead press
WednesdayPullLearn pulldowns and rows
FridayLegsSquat technique before load

Soreness in the first days is normal. It drops off sharply by week two or three.

Beginner mistakes.

  • Going too heavy in week one.
  • Changing exercises every week: keep the same ones so you can measure progress.
  • Not logging anything, then not knowing what weight to use next time.
  • Neglecting sleep and protein, which do a big part of the work.

Then apply double progression: more reps first, more weight next.

FAQ.

How long before I see results?

Strength goes up within the first weeks. Visible changes usually take 2 to 3 months of consistent training.

Should I train to failure from the start?

No. Keep 2 to 3 reps in reserve for the first weeks while you learn the movements.

What if I miss a session?

Just do the next session in order (Push, Pull, Legs) the next time you get to the gym.
