How many sets per muscle per week?.

Training volume mostly comes down to the number of hard sets per muscle per week. Here are simple benchmarks, and how to check them in your own program.

By Julien, developer of PPL Tracker · Updated November 19, 2026

The benchmarks.

LevelHard sets / muscle / week
Beginner (under a year)6 to 10
Intermediate10 to 16
Advanced12 to 20, depending on recovery

These are ballpark figures, not rules. More sets only help if you recover well and are still progressing. Below these numbers you still progress, just more slowly.

What counts as a "hard set"?

A set taken close to failure: 0 to 3 reps in reserve (see the RIR guide). Warm-up sets don't count. An easy set 6 reps short of failure barely counts.

How to count.

  • A set of an exercise that directly targets the muscle counts as 1.
  • For muscles working secondarily (triceps on bench press, biceps on rows), count about half a set.
  • Add it all up across the week, all sessions combined.

Example, chest in the beginner PPL program: bench press 3 sets + cable fly 2 sets = 5 sets per Push day. On 3 days that's 5 sets a week; on 6 days (Push A + Push B) it goes up to 10–12.

Spreading the volume.

Past 8 to 10 hard sets for the same muscle in a single session, quality often drops. That's why training each muscle twice a week makes sense as volume goes up: 6-day PPL, upper/lower, or a 4-day PPL.

FAQ.

Do warm-up sets count?

No. Only working sets taken to 0 to 3 reps from failure count toward volume.

Are more sets always better?

No. Beyond what you can recover from, extra volume adds nothing and can stall you. Increase gradually and check your weights keep going up.

How can I track my volume easily?

By logging every set. A tracker that shows weekly volume per muscle saves you doing the math by hand.
