How long should you rest between sets?.

Rest is part of the next set. Too short and you lose reps; too long and the session drags on. Here are benchmarks that work.

By Julien, developer of PPL Tracker · Updated November 19, 2026

The benchmarks.

Exercise typeSuggested rest
Heavy main lifts (squat, bench press, deadlift)2 to 3 min
Secondary compound lifts (rows, dumbbell press, leg press)90 s to 2 min
Isolation (curls, raises, extensions, calves)60 to 90 s

Why not cut it short.

For both muscle growth and strength, long enough rest on the big lifts lets you keep weights and reps high from set to set. Cutting rest feels like working harder, but often costs several reps on the following sets.

The right test.

Rest long enough that your next set is almost as good as the last one: dropping 1 or 2 reps is normal, dropping 4 means you went again too soon.

Saving time without losing quality.

  • Supersets on different muscles (say biceps then triceps): each muscle rests while the other works.
  • A timer: we almost always misjudge time. A timer that starts automatically after each set keeps you consistent.

FAQ.

Does short rest burn more fat?

Not really. Fat loss mostly comes down to diet. For lifting, use rest that keeps your sets high quality.

Should rest be the same every set?

Ideally yes, so you can compare sessions. That is what a timer is for.

What should I do while resting?

Log your set, drink, set up the next one. Avoid wandering off, which stretches your rest without you noticing.
