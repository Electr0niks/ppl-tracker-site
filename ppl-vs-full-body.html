PPL vs full body: which one should you pick?.

Both work. The real question isn't "which is best" but "which fits your schedule and your recovery".

By Julien, developer of PPL Tracker · Updated November 19, 2026

The two in brief.

  • Full body: the whole body every session, usually 2 to 3 times a week, with 1 or 2 exercises per muscle group.
  • Push Pull Legs: one session per movement type (push, pull, legs), 3 or 6 times a week, with more exercises per muscle.

The comparison.

The simple answer.

  • 2 days a week: full body, no question.
  • 3 days: both work. Full body for frequency, PPL if you prefer focused sessions.
  • 4 days: upper/lower, or a PPL set up for 4 days.
  • 5 to 6 days: PPL, that's where it shines.

The best program is the one you stick to for months. The split generator gives you the schedule for your available days.

FAQ.

Is PPL good for beginners?

Yes, as a 3-day split. Full body is a bit simpler for learning the lifts, but both let you progress fast in your first year.

Can I switch from full body to PPL?

Yes, it is a common next step once you have more time or want more volume per muscle. Keep the same main lifts so you can compare your numbers.

Which one builds more muscle?

With equal weekly volume, research shows no big difference. What matters is the number of hard sets per muscle and progressing over time.
