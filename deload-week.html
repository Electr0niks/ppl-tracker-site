The deload week: when and how.

Lift less to progress more: a deload is a deliberately lighter week that clears accumulated fatigue so you come back stronger.

By Julien, developer of PPL Tracker · Updated November 19, 2026

What it's for.

Over the weeks, fatigue builds up faster than it fades: joints, nervous system, motivation. A deload cuts your workload for a few days so that fatigue drops, without losing your gains. A common result: PRs the week after.

When to deload.

  • Planned: every 6 to 8 weeks for most lifters, a bit more often with age or very heavy training.
  • Reactive: if you stall or go backwards two sessions in a row on several lifts.
  • Other signs: nagging joint aches, worse sleep, usual weights feeling unusually heavy, wanting to skip sessions.

How to do it.

SettingDuring the deload
Number of setsAbout half
WeightsAbout 10% lighter (or the same with fewer sets)
Effort3 to 4 reps in reserve, never to failure
ExercisesThe same ones, to keep the groove
LengthOne week

Keep your usual training days: only the intensity drops. The following week, go back to your pre-deload weights and carry on with double progression.

Common mistakes.

  • Turning the deload into a full week off (that works too, but you lose a bit of rhythm).
  • "Using" the deload to test your maxes.
  • Coming back heavier than before the deload in the very first session.

FAQ.

Will I lose muscle during a deload?

No. One lighter week is not enough to lose muscle or strength; it actually helps you express them better afterwards.

Do beginners need deloads?

Less often, since the weights are still light. A deload every 8 to 10 weeks, or when you stall, is enough.

How do I know I am stalling?

By logging every session. If weights and reps stop moving on several lifts for two sessions, it is time.
