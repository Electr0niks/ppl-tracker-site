One rep max calculator.

Enter a set you've done (weight and reps): you get your estimated 1RM and the weight to use for every rep count. No need to test your max.

How it's calculated.

Your 1RM (one-rep max) is the heaviest weight you can lift once. The calculator averages two well-known formulas:

  • Epley: weight × (1 + reps / 30)
  • Brzycki: weight × 36 / (37 − reps)

Both hold up well between 2 and 10 reps. Beyond that, the estimate depends a lot on your muscular endurance. Table weights are rounded to 2.5 kg (5 lb), the smallest jump in most gyms.

What it's for.

Picking starting weights. Program says 3 × 8? Take the 8-rep weight from the table, then drop it slightly so you keep 1 or 2 reps in reserve.

Tracking progress without maxing out. Testing a true 1RM is tiring and risky. Comparing your estimated 1RM month to month tells you whether you're getting stronger.

FAQ.

Does it work for every exercise?

It works well for compound lifts (bench press, squat, deadlift, overhead press). It's less accurate on small isolation exercises.

Should I test my real 1RM?

You don't need to in order to progress. If you do, warm up properly, use a spotter and safety pins, and keep it to a few times a year.
