Push Pull Legs at home with dumbbells.

No gym? A pair of adjustable dumbbells is enough for a serious PPL. Here are the sessions, and more importantly how to keep progressing when weights are limited.

By Julien, developer of PPL Tracker · Updated November 19, 2026

Equipment.

  • Must-have: a pair of adjustable dumbbells (ideally up to 20–25 kg / 45–55 lb each).
  • Very useful: a bench (even a simple flat one), a doorway pull-up bar.
  • Bonus: resistance bands for face pulls and extensions.

Progressing when your dumbbells get too light.

At home you'll quickly max out your dumbbells, especially for legs. Before buying heavier, use:

  • More reps: go up to 15–20 reps while keeping 1 to 3 reps in reserve. Muscle grows in those ranges too.
  • Single-limb work: split squats, one-arm rows and presses double the load per limb.
  • Tempo: a 3-second lowering phase makes the same weight much harder.
  • Rest: slightly shorter rest on isolation moves.

To know whether you're progressing, log every set: double progression works at home just as well as in the gym.

FAQ.

Can you really build muscle at home?

Yes, as long as your sets are taken close to failure (1 to 3 reps in reserve) and you progress over time in weight or reps.

How many sessions per week?

3 sessions (Push, Pull, Legs) are enough to start. With more time, run the cycle twice a week.

No pull-up bar: how do I train my back?

Swap pull-ups for dumbbell rows, chest-supported rows on an incline bench and dumbbell pullovers.
