How Push Pull Legs works.

PPL groups muscles by the movement they perform:

  • Push: chest, shoulders, triceps.
  • Pull: back, rear delts, biceps.
  • Legs: quads, hamstrings, glutes, calves, abs.

Each session trains muscles that work together while the others recover. It's easy to schedule and works just as well at 3 or 6 sessions a week.

Beginner: 3 days a week.

Example: Monday Push, Wednesday Pull, Friday Legs, with a rest day between sessions. 45 to 60 minutes each.

How to progress.

Double progression

Every exercise has a rep range, for example 3 × 8–12. Keep the same weight and add reps from session to session. Once you hit the top of the range on every set (3 × 12), add weight: +2.5 kg (5 lb) for upper body, +5 kg (10 lb) for legs, then start again at the bottom of the range.

Effort

Finish most sets with 1 to 3 reps in reserve: you could do one or two more clean reps. There's no need to go to failure on the big lifts.

Warm-up

Before the first exercise: 5 minutes of light cardio, then 2 or 3 ramp-up sets (for example 50%, 70% and 85% of your working weight).

Deload

Every 6 to 8 weeks, or if you stall two sessions in a row on several lifts: train for one week with half the sets and about 10% less weight. You'll come back stronger.

Adjusting

No barbell? Use the dumbbell or machine version. An exercise hurts (pain, not fatigue)? Swap it for a variation. If you have a health condition, check with a professional before starting.

FAQ.

Is PPL good for beginners?

Yes, as a 3-day split. Each muscle is trained once a week, with enough volume to keep progressing for the first few months. Move to the 6-day version once your weights stop going up week to week.

How long should I run it?

At least 8 to 12 weeks before judging it. Log every session: if your weights or reps go up, it's working.

What if I only have 4 or 5 days?

Rotate Push, Pull, Legs, rest, Push, Pull… regardless of the weekday. Or use the split generator to find the layout that fits your schedule.

How do I pick my starting weights?

Start light in week one and find the weight that leaves 2 or 3 reps in reserve. The 1RM calculator also gives suggested weights for each rep count.
