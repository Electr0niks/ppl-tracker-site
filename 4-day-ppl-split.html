Running Push Pull Legs on 4 days a week.

PPL is built for 3 or 6 days. With 4 sessions you have to pick: rotate the cycle, add an upper-body day, or switch to upper/lower. Here are all three, no fuss.

By Julien, developer of PPL Tracker · Updated November 19, 2026

Option 1: the rolling rotation.

Run Push, Pull, Legs, Push, Pull, Legs… regardless of the weekday, 4 sessions a week. Each muscle gets trained about 1.3 times a week, and which session lands on Monday changes every week.

Best for: people who want to keep the exact PPL sessions. The catch: you need to know which session is next, which is where a log that lines up your next session helps a lot.

Option 2: PPL + an upper day.

Three classic PPL sessions plus a lighter fourth "upper body" day built from the main Push and Pull lifts. Legs get trained once, upper body twice.

MonTueThuFri
PushPullLegsUpper

Best for: people who mainly want to grow chest, back and arms. Add 2 or 3 leg sets at the end of the upper day if you want more balance.

Option 3: switch to upper / lower.

On 4 days, an upper/lower split is often the most logical choice: every muscle gets hit twice a week, with shorter sessions than PPL.

MonTueThuFri
UpperLowerUpperLower

Best for: people with 4 fixed days who want the most even frequency. The split generator shows this schedule for your days.

Which one to pick?

If you like PPL and your days change, go with the rolling rotation. If you want to emphasise the upper body, go with PPL + upper. If your 4 days are fixed and you just want the most balanced layout, go with upper/lower. Either way, what drives progress is logging your weights and trying to beat them a little each session.

FAQ.

Is a 4-day PPL less effective than 6 days?

It gives a bit less weekly volume, but it is still very effective, especially if you struggle to recover from 6 sessions. Consistency over months matters more than session count.

How long should each session be?

Plan 50 to 70 minutes including warm-up. Short on time? Drop an isolation set rather than a main lift.

Which exercises go in each session?

Use the ones from the free PPL program: bench and overhead press for Push, pulldowns and rows for Pull, squats and Romanian deadlifts for Legs.
